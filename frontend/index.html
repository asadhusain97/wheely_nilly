How the numbers work

Glossary

A field guide to the portfolio and screener metrics used throughout Wheely Nilly. Dashboard returns use matched, closed contracts; open credits remain unrealized.

Definitions for portfolio performance, capital, contracts, strategy settings, and options screener terms.

Strategy settings

Defaults, goals & ticker changes Settings layers
Defaults are the base rules. A goal changes only its filled-in values, and a ticker change applies only to that ticker and strategy. A lighter value is still coming from an earlier layer.
Effective value = ticker change, otherwise goal value, otherwise Default
Goal profiles Protect · Income · Exit · Acquire
Editable starting points for different intentions. Protect and Exit apply to covered calls, Acquire applies to cash-secured puts, and Income can apply to either. The names organize rules; they are not trading recommendations.
DTE range Time window
The shortest and longest time an option may have before expiration. An option must fall inside both limits to pass.
Minimum DTE ≤ days to expiration ≤ maximum DTE
Moneyness Strike / stock
How the strike price compares with the current stock price. 100% means they are equal; 90% means the strike is 10% below the stock; 110% means it is 10% above.
Moneyness = strike price ÷ stock price × 100%
Target delta range Absolute delta
Keeps options whose delta falls between your chosen limits. Wheely Nilly uses the size of delta, so a put delta of −0.25 is treated as 0.25. A blank limit leaves that side open.
Minimum delta ≤ |option delta| ≤ maximum delta
Liquidity rules Spread · OI · Volume
The spread measures the gap between buyers and sellers; a smaller gap usually means a more usable quote. Open interest counts contracts still open, while volume counts contracts traded today.
Midpoint = (bid + ask) ÷ 2
Spread % = (ask − bid) ÷ midpoint × 100
Passes when spread ≤ maximum, OI ≥ minimum, and volume ≥ minimum
Maximum quote age Freshness
The oldest price quote the screener may use. Older quotes are left out because the market may have moved since they were recorded.
Quote age = current time − quote timestamp
Minimum period return One option period
The smallest estimated return accepted over that option's own DTE. It is not an annualized rate. Net premium is the estimated option credit after the estimated fee.
Covered call = net premium ÷ current value of 100 shares
Cash-secured put = net premium ÷ (strike × 100 − net premium)
Net price guard Ticker setting
An optional per-share limit for one ticker. It can set the lowest net sale price for a covered call or the highest net purchase price for a cash-secured put. When monitoring begins using these settings, it will filter choices; it will not place an order.
Covered-call net sale price = strike + net premium per share
Put net purchase price = strike − net premium per share

Performance

Booked profit Booked P&L
The profit or loss from matched, closed short-option contracts after fees. Open premium is not booked until its contract closes.
Opening net credit + closing net cash flow
Return on collateral Return
Booked profit from qualified closed contracts divided by their combined entry collateral. A trade is excluded when its profit, collateral, or holding period cannot be established.
Qualified booked profit ÷ qualified entry collateral
Annualized return Annualized rate
A return scaled to one year. The portfolio dashboard weights qualified closed contracts by collateral and days held; same-day trades count as one day. The screener annualizes each candidate's estimated period return.
Dashboard: qualified booked profit × 365 ÷ Σ(collateral × days held)
Screener: net premium ÷ return collateral × 365 ÷ DTE
Capital velocity
Booked profit produced per $1,000 of collateral over a standardized 30-day period. It measures how efficiently deployed capital has generated realized profit.
Qualified booked profit ÷ collateral-days × 30 × $1,000
Premium capture
The share of positive opening credit retained as booked profit across closed contracts. Losses and buyback costs reduce the result.
Booked profit ÷ positive opening credit on closed contracts
Monthly booked P&L
Booked profit and loss grouped by the month each contract closed, then stacked by ticker.

Capital & contracts

Wheel capital
Capital currently associated with the wheel: cash securing open puts plus the broker cost basis of complete 100-share lots.
Open CSP collateral + share capital
CSP collateral Cash secured
Cash required to secure a short put at its strike. CSP means cash-secured put.
Strike × contract multiplier × open contracts
Share capital Shares committed
The broker-reported per-share cost basis of complete 100-share lots. For a covered call, only the shares committed to its contracts are used as collateral.
Broker cost basis per share × 100-share lots
Collateral Ticker view
Current CSP collateral plus share capital for a ticker. On an individual contract, it means the cash securing a put or the cost basis of shares committed to a call.
Open CSPs / open CCs
The number of current short put or short call contracts. CC means covered call; one standard contract represents 100 shares unless its multiplier says otherwise.
Contract multiplier
The number of shares one option contract controls. Standard equity options generally use 100; calculations use the contract's recorded multiplier when available.
Opening credit
The net cash received when a short option was opened, after recorded fees and allocated to the matched contract quantity.
Closing cash flow
The net cash from closing a contract. A buy-to-close cost is negative; expiration and assignment contribute zero closing option cash in booked option profit.
DTE Days to expiration
Calendar days remaining until the option expires. Next expiry is the earliest expiration among current short-option contracts.
Stock price
The latest available brokerage quote, falling back to the refreshed position price when a quote is unavailable.

Options screener

Premium Estimated
The candidate option's estimated per-share execution price. The screener uses the bid/ask midpoint only when the spread passes its configured limit.
Net premium = estimated premium × 100 − estimated fee
Delta
The estimated option-price change for a $1 move in the stock, also commonly read as a rough sensitivity measure. It comes from the provider when available, otherwise a Black–Scholes estimate.
